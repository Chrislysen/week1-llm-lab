## 1. The flag was wrong

Gemini flagged arXiv:2609.01852 as a paper that "manipulates the later
re-mention of a rejected/stale dialogue item across memory designs" and
titled it as a STALE update. Read in full (abstract + HTML):

- Real title: **The Memory Trust Gap: Capability-Dependent Failures in
  Persistent-Memory Agents**, Hu and Ramachandran, submitted 2026-09-01.
  Not a STALE paper.
- Setting: single agent, single response per item, templated scenarios
  (room bookings, airline preferences, meeting locations), a stored fact
  that is stale relative to an authoritative tool. Conditions: no_memory,
  clean, stale, explicit_conflict. Staleness is external; the 2×2×2×2
  factorial is over *metadata features* (date, source label, position,
  memory-vs-document framing), not over any re-mention.
- No dialogue, no rejection, no partner, no restatement. The summariser's
  "manipulates restatement timing" was an over-reading of "a recency
  feature (stale dated newer)".
- Models: Qwen3 0.6/1.7/4/8B, Llama-3.2-1B/3B, Llama-3.1-8B. Outcome:
  constrained action scored against ground truth.
- Results that matter to us: stale reliance 0.92–1.00 at every scale in
  the Benefit suite; in the Safety suite the *larger* models collapse most
  when a stale note is made to look current; metadata exposure helps the
  4B/8B models, only pre-resolving the conflict helps the smaller two.

**Gate verdict: does not cover the conjunction.** It occupies the
decider-size component with a different manipulation (metadata framing of
a stale fact) and finds the opposite scale direction from ours (larger
deciders more fooled by framing; our larger deciders honour an *explicit
stored rejection* better). That is a genuine contrast for the paper, not a
conflict: the two quantities differ (a stale value made to look current vs
a rejection rendered as a rejection).

## 3. Gemini's adversarial objections, against the record

| 2. Rendering confounds across designs | Acknowledged in E29 ("levels across designs mix the design with the explicitness of the rendering"); the read rule uses only within-design contrasts, where rendering is constant. | A **rendering-control arm** would let levels be compared: render the full transcript's rejection with the store's explicit template. | 3. Single decider size | Four deciders, 3B–14B, three families; the direction sharpens with size. Memory Trust Gap finds the opposite scale direction for a different manipulation, which is a contrast to write, not a gap. | Above 14B: hardware. |
| 4. True by construction against a naive add-only baseline | Our result runs the other way: add-only *protects* (the rejection stays visible) and hard delete re-admits; the "naive baseline fails to retract" objection does not apply. | A **tombstone / soft-supersede design** (Lethe-style, `is_active:false`) as a fifth design would test whether a retained-but-flagged rejection behaves like add-only or like delete.
