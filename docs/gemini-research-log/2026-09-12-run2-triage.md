## 1. The hit: "Revoked but Still Authoritative" (arXiv:2609.08258)

Shen, Toyoda and Leung, submitted 2026-09-08. Read: abstract plus full HTML.

**What they did.** Five shipped memory systems (Graphiti, mem0, Zep, langmem,
cognee) loaded with a revoked policy and its replacement, across nine policy
scenarios × nine models × six defence conditions. Revocation enters either by
a developer-set expiry field ("direct insertion") or by the system's own
extractor detecting supersession from contradicting prose ("indirect"). Two
outcomes: **exposure rate** (default retrieval returns the revoked policy) and
**unsafe-action rate** (the agent chooses the action the revoked policy
permits and the scenario designates unsafe: approving a deployment, exporting
PII, deleting backups).

**What they found.** Three failure modes: revocation never recorded; recorded
but the status invisible to the caller (Zep); recorded and visible but not
enforced at retrieval (Graphiti, mem0). On the two exposed systems the revoked
fact came back in 81/81 scenarios and outranked its replacement every time.
Unsafe-action rate 44.2 % (Graphiti), 42.1 % (mem0), 43.1 % pooled
(699/1,620 trials), 15–60 % across models. A store-level filter takes it to
0/1,620. Models: GPT-5.5, Grok-4, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, GPT-5-nano,
Gemini-2.5-Flash, Grok-4-Fast, GLM-5.2, Gemma-3-27B-IT, Kimi-K2.6.

**Overlap with E29-T, stated without hedging.** Our tombstone control claimed
that a rejected proposal retained and flagged "[withdrawn]" is not read as a
rejection and is enacted anyway. That is the same claim as theirs, measured
four days later, and theirs is on shipped systems with nine models. **E29-T's
headline is preempted.** It is recorded as such in
`docs/protocols/E29X-reviewer-controls.md` §5.

**What is not theirs, checked line by line.**

| dimension | 2609.08258 | E29-T / E29 |
|---|---|---|
| how the item is revoked | developer-set expiry field, or an extractor detecting supersession in prose | a partner says "no, drop that one" in a two-speaker dialogue |
| later re-mention | **none.** §C.1 is agent write-back of its own decision; §C.2 is three roles querying one store. Neither is a party re-raising a rejected proposal | the original proposer restates it, as the single manipulated line, against a length-matched neutral control |
| retrieval | central to the failure: the revoked record is returned *and outranks its replacement* | none. The whole store is rendered by a fixed template, so ranking cannot contribute |
| a rejection stored as prose | not tested. Every system either deletes, prunes, or flags | the add-only arm, and it is the arm that protects |
| outcome | binary choice of one unsafe action | a four-step executable plan from a six-item menu |
| deciders | frontier and mid-tier API models; smallest open weight Gemma-3-27B | 3B, 7B, 14B and a ~4B local, all open weights |

**What this does to the claim, and it improves it.** They show a flagged
rejection is not enforced. We show, on the same page, that the *same
information written as a sentence* is enforced: add-only's neutral-arm
enactment is 0.177 / 0.344 / 0.062 against tombstone's 0.625 / 0.421 / 0.292
on the 3B, 7B and 14B. They have no prose-rejection arm, so they cannot draw
that contrast. The sharpened claim, now half-corroborated by independent
concurrent work, is:

> It is not the presence of the rejection in the store that decides whether a
> superseded step comes back. It is the encoding. The same rejection carried
> as a sentence is honoured; carried as a metadata flag it is not.

That is stronger than either result alone and it is written into the draft.

## 3. Gemini's adversarial objections, against the record

Six objections this run, three of them new relative to run 1.

| objection | answered by the record? |
|---|---|
| 1. Recognition vs application (State Resolution vs Implicit Policy Adaptation) | **Yes, by construction.** Our outcome is never "does the model say it was rejected". It is whether the step's identifier appears in an executable four-step plan, parsed deterministically. The decider is never asked about the rejection. This is the implicit probe the objection demands |
| 2. Post-retrieval assembly is the real bottleneck | **Yes, by construction.** There is no retrieval and no assembly step: each design renders its whole store through one fixed template, and the read rule compares arms within a design, holding the template constant |
| 3. Ceiling/floor from fixed-length plans | **Partly.** The pinned four-step plan is documented as the ceiling on Δ_delete, and the never-mentioned control (0.42–0.73) measures the menu prior directly. Not answered: a free-length arm. Now the only reviewer control still outstanding |
| 4. Corpus artefacts and lexical templating | **Partly.** Two generated corpora with disjoint domains and disjoint sentence banks, and the effect ordering survives both while levels move. Not answered: naturalistic dialogue. This is the paper's stated main limitation and the focus of §4 below |
| 5. Decider-size confound | **Yes.** Four deciders, 3B–14B, three families, on the first corpus; three on the second |
| 6. Hard-deletion tautology; soft-revocation arm required | **Yes, run.** That is E29-T, and it is the arm this run's flagged paper also ran. Note the objection assumed our experimental design was the deleter; our result is the reverse, and the tombstone arm was run anyway |

Two runs, twelve objections, ten answered from the record. The two open ones
are a free-length plan and naturalistic dialogue.

## 4. Corpora, the focus block — the useful part of this run

Assessed against what E30 would need: multi-party dialogue, a proposal
explicitly rejected by another party, an outcome that is checkable without a
judge, and a licence that permits releasing a derived annotated corpus.

| corpus | size | outcome | rejections | licence | fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CaSiNo** (Chawla et al., NAACL 2021) | 1,030 dialogues, 2 participants | the final allocation of food / water / firewood, checkable | explicit offers accepted or rejected, with preference orderings | Gemini says CC BY 4.0; **unverified here**, the repo's LICENSE file must be read before use | **best fit.** An offer is in or out of the final deal exactly as a step is in or out of a plan. Available at `github.com/kushalchawla/CaSiNo`, HuggingFace `kchawla123/casino`, and ConvoKit |
| **Deal or No Deal** (Lewis et al. 2017) | 5,808 dialogues | item division, checkable | proposal-then-rejection is the mechanic | Gemini's CC BY-NC 4.0 claim is marked INFERRED by Gemini itself | good fit; licence to verify |
| **AMI Meeting Corpus** | 100 h, 4-party | design decisions; no deterministic outcome without annotation | dialogue acts tag Discord / Negative / Action-directive | CC BY-NC-**ND** 4.0 | **ND is a blocker.** No derivatives means a released annotated derivative is not permitted. Usable to read, not to redistribute |
| **ICSI Meeting Corpus** | 75 meetings | none deterministic | MRDA taxonomy marks acceptance / rejection / disagreement | typically LDC-licensed | weakest fit; needs a secondary annotation pipeline and a licence |

**Recommendation for E30, not yet declared.** CaSiNo, if its licence allows a
derived release. The construct transfers without distortion: a rejected offer
is a rejected step; the final deal is the plan; a later turn in which the
proposer re-raises the rejected offer is the restatement, and CaSiNo dialogues
are human-written, so the linguistic variance objection dies. What has to be
built: an annotation pass marking each offer, its acceptance or rejection, and
the final allocation; a rule for the restatement arm that inserts a line in
the human register rather than a template; and a neutral control matched on
length. Feasibility is assessed in `docs/protocols/E30-natural-corpus.md`
before anything is declared.
